**The user asked for bold wording where the evidence carries it** (2026-10-08). The rule applied here:
- **Established:** the strongest wording the evidence supports, provided the result holds:
  - held out, forward in time (day-half blocks);
  - in ≥ 5/6 rats;
  - and, where per-session thresholds could matter, under the fixed-threshold scores too.
- **Candidate:** reported plainly, with its main alternative named.
- **Mechanism words** (scheduler, gate, homeostat) stay interpretations.

**Common to all claims:**
- **Measurement.** Automatic 1-s scores: FINAL = `imu_remclean` + one substitution. There is no manual epoch scoring (κ).
- **Data.** 6 rats, outdoor paddock, 08-30 → 09-11 19:40.
- **Units of replication.** Rats (6) and day-half blocks (22).
- **Validation.** Forward chaining unless stated.
- **Δ.** Held-out log-loss per 10-s NREM step. It is a reliability measure; effect sizes are given on interpretable
  scales.

- **Established.**
  - In the field, when REM starts is set by the NREM accumulated since the last REM — a count that brief awakenings do
    not erase.
  - The night limits how often REM starts, not how long it lasts.
  - Over the 12 days the rats shifted sleep from night into day.
- **Candidate (strong).**
  - (Established since 2026-10-09: a wake of 7 min or more is not resumed — #6a.)
  - At night, longer wakes push REM back further.
  - (Established since 2026-10-09, #14b: at the same count and prior wake the night lowers the per-step REM-entry
    rate to about a third; model-free, about half the REM bouts per NREM hour.)
  - REM missed before a morning round is repaid within hours, specifically REM.
- **Model (2026-10-09, Steps A–B).** One count of NREM since the last REM, emptied by each REM and fading over a wake
  with a time constant of ~5–10 min, predicts NREM endings better than the multi-term model (#32–#34; #6a Established).
- **Day vs night (2026-10-09, Step C).** At the same count and the same prior wake the night lowers REM entry to about
  a third (#14b Established); no switch at dusk or dawn is detectable beyond the smooth daily curve (#35); housemates'
  sleep predicts a rat's own transitions (#38); the 12-day drift is not weather or time outside (#39).
- **The rules suffice (2026-10-09, Step E).** Simulated from the count, the daily clock and a brief arousal before REM,
  conditional on the observed waking, the model reproduces daily REM and NREM and their day-to-day variation without any
  budget (#40), and beats a momentum model (#41); an NREM homeostat adds nothing (#42). The one thing it does not produce is
  the REM repayment after handling (#43).
- **Sleep stability (2026-10-09, part S).** The model predicts the amounts of sleep after a battery round, not its
  stability: for about an hour after a round NREM ends in waking ~1.3–1.4× as often as predicted, in all six rats
  (#44), and the first REM comes later in wall time than simulated (#47); and it cannot tell which movements will wake
  (#45). Part P (2026-10-09): a decaying arousal fitted on waking alone captures the waking excess (#56) but not the
  REM delay (#57). Part T: a delayed calm phase of the tested form adds nothing held out (#58); the recovery the model
  misses is mostly NREM; FINAL's extra REM is base-dependent and pass2med's REM debt slope is the only robust REM
  residual (#43), so no REM-need variable was added. Part L: a post-round recording starts at the release into the
  paddock and half open with ~2 min scored NREM (#59); the first-REM delay sits mostly in those records, the first REM
  entry is not specially mispredicted (#61), and only an evening NREM-clock delay on fixed thresholds remains (#60).
  Part H (house-entry clock): the opening NREM is the rat lying motionless in the field (#62); waking is highest in the field, raised for
  the first hour in the house and below the model 1–3 h after entry (#63); the morning first REM after entry is
  reproduced, the evening one needs more NREM on both scorings (#60).
- **Inside the sleep sequence (2026-10-09, part F1).** The same count that sets REM entry also predicts how long the
  next REM lasts (#48); brief awakenings are most frequent right after a REM (#51); REM already taken that day adds
  nothing (#52); intervals between REMs do not shorten within an episode (#53). Night REM ends in waking more often
  (#49) and quick returns to REM are rarer at night (#50) — both Candidate.
- **Ripples (2026-10-09, next stage LFP).** NREM ripples add no REM-scheduling information beyond the count (#54); the
  ripple counter's small gain comes from ripple-poor short NREM bouts between REM bouts (#55).
- **Organising idea (interpretation).** Only REM entry is regulated, by this count and a smooth daily curve (#35); a
  separate REM-repayment process appears only after handled interruptions (#43). Daily totals may be regular because the inputs are regular, not because a
  budget is enforced. This is tested by the scheduler model of the plan.
- **Not shown.** A day-to-day 24-h balance (#13 withdrawn), a fixed timetable, an NREM rebound proportional to wake
  length, and outside causes of awakenings.
