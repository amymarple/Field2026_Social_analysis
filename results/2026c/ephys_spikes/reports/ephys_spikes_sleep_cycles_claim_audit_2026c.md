Produced with `/scientific-report-promotion` for the first run of `ephys/sleep_cycles.py`.
- Ledger: [ephys_spikes_sleep_cycles_2026c.md](ephys_spikes_sleep_cycles_2026c.md),

- This audit sets **status and allowed wording** only; it is not a narrative.
- The synthetic `--selftest` verifies code behaviour, not biology.

## Common to every claim

**Measurement**
- The states are **automatic** SleepScoreMaster scores. LFP slow wave and theta are thresholded per session, with the
  head-IMU acceleration in the EMG slot, then the `imu_remclean` post-rules:
  - no REM after > 10 s of wake;
  - no REM while the head moves ≥ 3 of 5 s;
  - REM ≥ 7 s.
- The user's review was a strict **visual screen** of overview figures, not manual scoring, so there is no epoch-level
  accuracy (κ) yet.

**Design**
- 6 implanted rats (SF07–SF12) in a semi-natural paddock, 08-31 → 09-12. 168 sessions, 1281 h after exclusions.
- The light phase runs from the computed sunrise to sunset.
- The units of replication are **dates** (12 light-phase folds) and **animals** (6), not cycles or steps.

**Outcome space of the transition model**
- It covers NREM → REM and NREM → Wake from NREM bouts that follow a REM of known length.
- **14.6 % of all REM entries (670 / 4597) come directly from brief wake (≤ 10 s, allowed by the post-rules) and are
  outside the model.**
- Wake → REM after > 10 s is excluded by construction.

## Stop conditions hit

- **#8** flips sign under the pre-post-rule states, so it is not promotable in its general form.
- **#7**: the full night-transfer number (−4.9 %) is confounded by the baseline's missing time-of-day term; only the
  no-wake version (−1.1 %) may be cited as transfer.
- **Outcome space.** REM entries from brief wake (14.6 %) are outside the transition model. #1 – #5 are scoped to
  NREM-origin transitions.
- **Corrected before this audit.** The night-transfer "relative to M0" values had used the light-phase baseline
  (−5.4 % / −1.2 %); the correct night baseline gives −4.9 % / −1.1 % (driver fixed, report regenerated).
